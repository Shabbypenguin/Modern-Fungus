# How to Play

> One night in the lab, after the biologists have gone home, an experiment takes on a life of its own. Be the last fungus alive.

## Setting up a game
1. Pick **Classic** (the 1994 game, up to 4 players) or **Modern** (up to 16 players). New players should start with Classic.
2. Pick a **board**. Classic 20 is the original size. Bigger boards mean longer games.
3. In Modern, pick a **Game speed**. Faster games grow teeth more often and give bigger bites sooner.
4. Fill the seats with **Human** or **CPU** players. Several humans can share one screen and take turns.
5. Press **Begin**.

To play people on other devices, use **Play online** instead (see [[Online Play]]).

## The screen
- **Board:** your fungus starts as a single cell with a face, its **head**.
- **Player cards** (sidebar): each player's name, colour and bites. The current player's card is outlined.
- **Piece preview:** the piece you're placing this turn. Click or tap it to rotate.
- **Timer bar** (when a turn limit is set): how much time is left this turn.
- **Bite info:** how big a fungus has to be for big and monster bites in this game.
- **Status line:** what just happened, and what to do next.

## Your turn, step by step
1. **Look at your piece.** It's random, one of 11 shapes from a single cell to a 4-long bar.
2. **(Optional) Bite first.** If you have bites, press **Bite** (or `B`), press on a marked enemy cell next to your fungus, and drag in a straight line. Biting costs one bite and **doesn't end your turn**, so you can bite several times, then still place.
3. **Rotate** with `Q`/`E`, `Space`, right-click, or by tapping the preview.
4. **Place** the piece so it touches your fungus along an **edge**. Corners don't count.
5. The turn passes to the next player. If you can't or won't move, press **Pass** (`G`).

## Growing and capturing
- **Capture:** after you place, look along all 8 directions (across, down, and diagonally) from each new cell. If enemy cells sit in a line with your own cell at the far end, the nearest of those enemy cells becomes yours. The check then runs again from that cell, so one move can set off a chain reaction.
- **Diagonal captures** only survive if they connect to your fungus along an edge. Otherwise they're destroyed.
- **Stay connected:** every cell must connect back to its head along edges. When a capture or bite cuts part of a fungus off, that part shrivels and dies.
- **Heads:** capture an enemy's head and that player is out. You inherit **all their cells and all their bites**.

## Teeth and bites
- **Teeth** are the white triangles on the board. Grow onto one to collect it: **+1 bite**.
- Every so often **new teeth grow** and everyone alive gets +1 bite. In Classic that's every 8–13 rounds.
- **Bite size grows with your fungus:**
  - Bites eat 1 cell.
  - Big bites eat up to 2 cells in a line.
  - Monster bites eat up to 3 cells in a line.
  - Classic gets big bites at 80 cells and monster at 160. Modern shows its numbers in the sidebar.
- Bites can't eat heads. A bite of any length costs one bite.

## Winning
- **Last fungus alive wins.**
- If everyone passes for a long stretch (2 turns per player in a row), the game ends and the **largest fungus wins**.
- Local wins and losses go into **Best fungal growths** on the home screen.

## Strategy tips
- **Grow toward teeth early.** Bites are the only way through a wall of enemy fungus.
- **Bite, then place.** A bite opens a gap; the piece you place afterwards can fill it and capture right away. That combo is the point of bites.
- **Guard your head.** An enemy who reaches your head with a line takes everything. Keep a buffer of your own cells around it.
- **Thin necks are weak points.** One bite through a one-cell-wide connection kills everything beyond it. That works on enemies too.
- **Chains win games.** Before placing, look for spots where one capture lines up the next.
- **Don't overextend.** Long tendrils are easy to cut off.
- **Grow big for bigger bites.** Monster bites cut 3-deep and make fungus a lot harder to defend.
